# Fresh reading-estimator stress evaluation — 200 rounds

**Result: 200 fresh synthetic rounds completed; not validated for real readers.** The four audited changes were in place before the run: two in production and two in the candidate adapter. On 1,982,800 main cases, the fixed production baseline beats the saved legacy comparator overall, but the experimental recent/prior candidate loses to the fixed baseline at both the fitted weight (0.979) and the pre-registered 0.5 weight. The candidate is rejected on this evidence. Generated by `python3 tools/cde/write_fresh_200_report.py` (exit 0).

This report supplements the [100-round report](fresh-reading-stress-100-2026-10-30.md) and the [root-cause audit](fresh-stress-root-cause-2026-10-30.md). It does not replace their historical results and does not turn generated outcomes into measurements of people.

## What was requested and done

The owner asked for every audited defect to be fixed, then for 200 fresh rounds to show what remained. Four audited changes were already present when this run began and were unchanged during it; the first two are production code, the last two are the candidate adapter’s own rules:

1. **Per-book normalization before pooling.** [`crossBookReadingSpeed`](../../AsterismaV2.5/Engines/AnalyticsEngine.swift#L83) computes each book’s robust mean first, then combines the book paces with [`consensusSeconds`](../../AsterismaV2.5/Engines/AnalyticsEngine.swift#L459). Two books that disagree by more than 3× use the faster one; three or more use the median.
2. **Contamination-resistant ceiling.** [`robustMeanSeconds`](../../AsterismaV2.5/Engines/AnalyticsEngine.swift#L424) retains values up to [`max(median, lowerQuartile × 3)`](../../AsterismaV2.5/Engines/AnalyticsEngine.swift#L444), instead of anchoring the ceiling to a median that moves with the contamination.
3. **Three-sample minimum for the candidate’s recent pace.** [`minimumRecentTimings`](../../AsterismaV2.5Tests/Engines/AdversarialEvaluationTests.swift#L71) is 3; two timings are no longer treated as a pace. The production estimator’s own five-timing threshold is unchanged.
4. **Two-sided plausibility guard.** The candidate falls back when the recent/prior ratio is outside [0.35–3.0](../../AsterismaV2.5Tests/Engines/AdversarialEvaluationTests.swift#L73), not only when the target is implausibly fast.

The run contains 40 batches of five rounds, 9,914 main cases per round, plus 200 demo rounds of 16 personas: **1,982,800 main predictions and 3,200 demo predictions**, 1,986,000 in total. These are case predictions, not independent people or independent readers. Inputs, truth, Swift predictions, grades, result bundles and logs were recorded in [the evidence archive](evidence/fresh-stress-evidence-archive-2026-10-31.md) before the raw tree was deleted on 2026-10-31.

**Provenance limitation.** The driver was interrupted after batch 175 and resumed at batch 176. The resumable driver skipped already graded batches, reused the prepared input for the interrupted batch, and ran the demo sweep once. All 40 graded files passed the verifier, and every saved `candidate_seconds` value matches the same rule; this is not evidence of one clean uninterrupted invocation.

## Method

The **production baseline** is the actual Swift `EstimationEngine` after the two production fixes, invoked through the existing adversarial adapter. The **legacy comparator** (`legacy`) is the adapter’s saved same-case reference. For targets with fewer than five timings it uses the old pooled formula: one cross-book median, the old median-3× ceiling, one mean. For targets with at least five timings it calls the current `AnalyticsEngine.readingSpeed`, which already contains the fixed per-book policy. It is therefore a hybrid, not a full reproduction of the retired estimator. It isolates the pooling/consensus change on no-personal-speed cases; it does not isolate the ceiling change on personal-speed cases, where `legacy` equals the baseline by construction.

The **experimental candidate** blends two values when both exist: the robust mean of the last eight usable target timings (`recent`) and the fixed cross-book consensus over the other books (`prior`). It requires at least three recent timings and applies the two-sided 0.35–3.0 guard. When `recent` is absent it returns `prior`; when `prior` is absent it returns `recent`; when the guard rejects `recent` it returns `prior`. It is not a production formula change.

The blend weight was fitted only on rounds 1–100, by minimizing mean absolute percentage error over the exact piecewise-linear objective, and then frozen for rounds 101–200 and the demo profiles. The fitted weight is **0.979013257067**, effectively recent-only. Calibration used 244,514 eligible cases (24.66% of the 991,400 development cases). That subset includes only cases where both `recent` and `prior` exist and the guard passes; it excludes every sparse fallback and every guard rejection. The pre-registered 0.5 blend is reported separately so the goalposts are visible.

Absolute percentage error is `100 × |prediction − synthetic target| / synthetic target`. A spike is an absolute error greater than both 300 seconds and 75% of the synthetic target; this is an investigation rule, not a validated pass/fail threshold. The 90th percentile is the sorted value at Python index `round((n − 1) × 0.9)`. Percentages are rounded here; full precision is preserved as compressed summaries in [the evidence archive](evidence/fresh-stress-evidence-archive-2026-10-31.md).

### Sparse targets: the fallback discards the target’s own book

`sparse` contains 480,800 cases; the candidate differs on 468,604. Its median rises from 16.67% to 27.11%, its mean from 22.76% to 36.17%, and its spikes from 12,206 to 39,528. The saved sample counts explain the mechanism: 484,600 cases have exactly two target timings and 472,404 of them differ. With fewer than three timings `recent` is absent by design, so the candidate returns `prior` — the consensus over the *other* books. The production baseline instead uses the fixed `crossBookReadingSpeed` over all books, which includes the target book and therefore its two timings. The three-sample rule stops the old two-sample spike, but the chosen fallback throws away the only target evidence available.

This is visible in the tail. The 100-round candidate’s worst main case was 3,628.89% from a mean of two interrupted intervals; that shape is gone from the 200-round differing set. The new worst differing case is 873.36%. The overall maximum remains 2596.05%, a zero-history cold start that is identical in both models.

### Same-book targets: a last-eight window is noisier than the full history

`same_book` contains 477,600 cases; the candidate differs on 476,000. The median moves from 5.48% to 6.89%, the mean from 7.64% to 14.82%, and spikes from 158 to 21,060. When the reader’s pace is stationary, restricting the estimate to the last eight usable timings adds variance and discards the older sample; it does not add information. The production baseline’s full-history robust mean is the better estimator on this regime.

### Corpus: the guard can prefer a contaminated prior

`corpus` contains 59,600 cases; the candidate differs on 53,800. Its mean rises from 28.81% to 59.51% and its spikes from 6,027 to 7,763. The largest differing cases share one shape: `recent` is close to the baseline and the synthetic target, while `prior` is roughly ten times larger; the guard sees a recent/prior ratio below 0.35, treats the target as the implausible one, and returns the contaminated prior.

The guard assumes that disagreement means the target is contaminated. That assumption is not safe across books with different page scales: the target can have 11–18 usable timings while the cross-book prior is the outlier. The fallback should not be the prior when a trusted recent pace exists.

### The unrelated world and the one family where the candidate wins

In `unrelated`, where the target has a hidden response effect the estimator cannot see, the candidate’s median is 52.09% against 27.24% and its spikes are 107,202 against 66,971. A prior from other books cannot identify a hidden effect; the loss here is expected.

`adversarial` is the exception: the candidate’s median is 10.04% against 18.38%, and it has 0 spikes against 200. The adversarial slot is constructed so that the designed reversal makes a prior fallback useful. This is evidence that the rule can win where the prior is the right reference, not evidence that it wins in general.

## Calibration: the fit answers a narrower question than deployment

| Weight | Development objective, mean APE % |
|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 32.17 |
| 0.25 | 24.81 |
| 0.5 | 18.05 |
| 0.75 | 12.46 |
| 1.0 | 9.98 |

The objective decreases monotonically toward recent-only: the prior adds essentially nothing on the 244,514 cases where both values pass the guard. That is why the fitted weight is 0.979, and why the 0.5 blend is worse (26.70% median against 23.09% at the fitted weight). But the fit never sees the 2-sample fallbacks or the guard rejections, which are where the candidate loses most of its cases. A weight selected on the eligible subset cannot justify the rule’s behaviour on the excluded majority of differences.

## Limitations and next decision

The outcomes are generated assumptions, not measurements of real readers. The recipes and book sets are reused; only the seeds are new. The corpus uses stored package scores, not the books or their text. The adapter receives closed intervals, whereas live sessions and SQLite summary reload can differ. The `legacy` comparator is a hybrid: it uses the old pooled formula only for targets with fewer than five timings, and the current per-book policy otherwise, so `same_book` is identical to the baseline by construction and the ceiling change is not isolated. The guard compares seconds per page across books whose page scales differ, which the corpus cases expose. The demo target is generated elapsed time, not active reading time. The run resumed after an interruption, and the calibration subset excludes the cases where the candidate most often differs. There is no independent hand grading and no second reader. No production weight was changed by this analysis, and nothing was committed or pushed.

**Decision.** Keep the two fixed production behaviours. Reject the recent/prior candidate at both weights, including its three-sample minimum and two-sided guard as deployed rules. The next experiment, if the owner wants one, should fall back to the fixed baseline when `recent` is missing or rejected, shrink a recent estimate toward the target’s full history rather than toward a cross-book prior, and calibrate on the cases where the rule actually changes the answer. That experiment has not been run; it is a hypothesis, not a result.
